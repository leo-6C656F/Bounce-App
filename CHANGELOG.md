# Changelog

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Recordings can be joined into one.** A Plaud closes its file every time you stop recording, so half an hour of dictation captured in bursts arrives as thirty separate rows you can't summarise or read as one train of thought. **Join with…** in a recording's menu picks the other parts and combines them into a single recording with a single transcript — joined oldest first, with a chapter heading at each seam so you can still see what came from where. Highlights, tasks and speaker names come across; speakers are renumbered rather than merged, since "Speaker 1" in two recordings isn't the same person. The originals are kept unless you say otherwise. The audio is copied, never re-encoded, so it's bit-for-bit the same as what your recorder captured.
- **Link a recording to an existing one while it's still running.** **Continue a recording…** on the live screen says "this is the rest of that" — when it syncs, the two are joined automatically, transcribed as one, and the parts are removed. Do it again on the next burst and it keeps folding into the same recording. If either half hasn't finished syncing yet, it waits.
- **A reminder for the simpler case:** if you just want to break for a moment, **pause** already keeps everything in one recording — from the app, your watch, the Lock Screen or the Live Activity. Joining is for when recording actually stopped.

- **Chapters in a transcript.** After a recording is transcribed, Apple Intelligence names the sections of the conversation and marks where each begins, so a long meeting gets headings and a row of chips you can tap to jump straight to a topic. There's a search box for finding a phrase inside the transcript, and speakers now carry a coloured initial rather than a repeated "Speaker 2". Recordings under two minutes aren't chaptered — there's only one topic — and on an iPhone without Apple Intelligence the transcript reads as it always did. A **Find chapters** button chapters recordings that were transcribed before this existed. All on device.

- **An Apple Watch app.** Start, stop, pause and mark a moment from your wrist, plus a Sync button and the recorder's battery and connection state at a glance. It's a remote control for the iPhone, which does the talking to your recorder — so it works when the phone is in a pocket, in a bag, or with Bounce not even open, because a tap wakes the app in the background. When the phone can't be reached, or the recorder isn't connected, the watch says which rather than appearing to do nothing. No transcripts, titles or locations are sent to the watch.

- **Meeting series — recurring meetings read as one continuing conversation.** Group the sessions of a weekly standup or a monthly review into a series, and each new session gets a **"Since last time"** recap written against everything before it: what moved on, what was decided, what's still open. The series itself keeps a running **"Where this stands"** note that's rewritten after every session. Library gains a Series mode listing them all.
- **Recurring calendar events group themselves.** If a recording matches a repeating event in your calendar (with calendar matching on), it joins that meeting's series automatically — sessions months apart included, because the grouping uses the calendar's stable identifier rather than the meeting's name. Renaming the meeting doesn't split the series in two.
- **Link a recording to a meeting by hand, and have it stick.** A recording now shows the calendar meeting it belongs to, with a **Link to a meeting** picker that lists the meetings from your calendars **before, during and after** the recording — tap one to link it. Automatic matching now only links a meeting when it's confident and unambiguous; when it isn't sure (two back-to-back meetings, a short recording, or a meeting it couldn't find), it leaves the choice to you rather than guessing. A meeting you pick — or clear — by hand is remembered and never overwritten by a later re-transcription. Linking a recurring meeting also joins its series.
- **Series can be built from recordings you already have.** "Read all sessions" goes back over a series' existing transcripts oldest-first and builds the running note from them, with a progress bar — so the feature works on an established library rather than only on recordings made from now on.
- **The continuity prompt is editable** like every other AI instruction, in Settings › AI › Prompts. All of it runs on device; nothing is uploaded.

- **Recordings can be tagged with where they happened, and seen on a map.** Switch on Settings › Integrations & Delivery › Location, and Bounce takes a single location reading when your recorder starts recording — the moment your iPhone is in range of it, so that really is where the meeting was. Library gains a Map mode alongside List and Calendar; the map honours whatever search, category and tag filters are already applied, and tapping a pin opens the recording. Every location is editable: search for a place, tap the map, use your current location, or remove it. Off by default, and permission is asked the first time you switch it on.
- **Two fallbacks for recordings made away from your iPhone.** A Plaud records on its own, so if your phone wasn't in range Bounce uses the location of the calendar event the recording matched (when calendar matching is on), or — only for a recording that synced within half an hour of ending — where you were when the file transferred. That last one is labelled **"Synced here — approximate"** everywhere it appears, and an older recording gets no location at all rather than a confident pin in the wrong place.
- **Locations stay on this iPhone.** They are never included in webhook or folder deliveries, never logged, and turning the setting back off leaves the ones you already have alone rather than erasing them.
- **Settings can be changed from the desktop view.** The browser now has a full settings page and a documented `GET`/`PATCH /api/settings` behind it, covering transcription, AI, delivery and recorder preferences. **Your paired browser only** — an API token is refused on both verbs, including reading, because the snapshot carries your delivery webhook URL. Secrets are write-only: the page shows whether a Soniox key or webhook secret is *set*, never the value. A change either applies completely or not at all. Two things it deliberately can't do: touch the recorder (those are Bluetooth commands, not preferences) and grant a permission — calendar and notification toggles can only be switched off from the browser, since only the phone can ask. Full reference in [docs/api.md](docs/api.md).
- **The desktop view documents its own API.** A new API tab lists every endpoint and every MCP tool, built from what the server actually reports rather than from a copy that can go stale, so it always describes the build you're running. The `curl` examples and the Claude Desktop config block come pre-filled with your phone's real address and port, ready to paste. Your token is never shown — it appears once when you generate it and nowhere else — so the examples carry a placeholder instead.
- **An agenda on a recording.** Type the topics you meant to cover and Bounce ticks them off as they turn up in the transcript. Matching needs every significant word of a topic to actually appear, and ticking a row by hand makes it yours — automatic matching stops second-guessing it from then on. Nothing appears until you add a topic.
- **An executive brief on the recording screen**, with one-tap Markdown copy covering the title, date, duration, category, summary and action items.
- **A mini player above the tab bar.** Audio now keeps playing when you leave a recording, with play/pause and dismiss from any tab — previously it stopped on any tab switch because there was nowhere else to control it from.
- **Category filter chips in the Library**, and a segmented Pending/Completed/All filter with search and quick-add in Tasks.
- **Suggested questions in Ask** and in a recording's Ask section, as one-tap starting points, plus a copy button on any answer.
- **Desktop view survives switching apps.** It used to stop the moment Bounce left the screen, because iOS suspends a backgrounded app and the connection dies with it. Bounce now keeps itself running while desktop view is on, so the transcript on your monitor keeps updating while you're in another app or the phone is locked — for up to two hours at a stretch, after which it switches off rather than sitting there awake. It uses more battery while on, and the browser reconnects on its own if anything does interrupt it. Even without this, desktop view now stays up for as long as a recording is in progress.
- **Start and Stop Desktop View are Shortcuts actions**, so an automation can turn it on without you opening Bounce — on joining your desk Wi-Fi, tapping an NFC sticker, at a time of day, or when your recorder connects. Starting reports the address and pairing code back, or says why it couldn't. Nothing outside the phone can start Bounce when it isn't running — no app on iOS can be reached over the network while it's closed — but the phone can launch it for you, and it now stays running once it has.

- **Every AI instruction is editable.** Settings › AI › Prompts exposes the seven prompts Bounce sends to the on-device model — how recordings are categorised and titled, what counts as a task, how spoken deadlines become dates, how summaries and Ask are grounded — with a per-prompt Reset and a Reset all. Prompts use placeholders like `{transcript}`; the editor lists the ones each prompt needs, inserts them with a tap, and warns if one is missing. Editing a prompt changes how well that feature works, but it can't break the app: the output's structure is enforced separately, and every default is one tap away.
- **Tasks are proposals you review, then send — nothing is added automatically.** Action items pulled from a recording collect in the Tasks tab as candidates. Tap **Send** on a task (or **Send all** for a recording) and it goes to whatever destinations you've turned on; a "Sent" badge marks the ones on their way. This replaces automatically mirroring every task the moment a destination was switched on, which added tasks to Apple Reminders you never asked for.
- **Tasks can go to Reminders, your calendar, or a webhook** — pick any combination in Settings › Integrations & Delivery. Reminders keeps completion in sync both ways. Calendar adds an event for tasks that have a deadline (and only those — an undated task belongs in Reminders); Bounce only ever touches events it created. The webhook POSTs one small JSON object per task so you can route into Todoist, Things, Linear or anything else — **the transcript is never included**. All are off until you turn them on, and only tasks you send are ever written.
- **Deadlines are worked out into real dates.** "By Friday" becomes an actual date, because Bounce tells the AI what day the recording was made and which weekday that was. The phrase you said is kept alongside it, and a date that can't be worked out confidently is left off rather than guessed — so a task keeps its wording either way. A sent task carries that date through: its reminder alerts on the day, its calendar event lands there, and the webhook includes it.
- **Built-in summary templates can be deleted**, with "Restore built-in templates" to bring them back — the same as categories. Your edits to a template are kept if you delete and restore it.
- **A local API and an MCP server**, so scripts and AI agents can read your library over your own network. Generate a token in Settings › Desktop view › API access and point Claude Desktop at it: it can then search your recordings, read transcripts and summaries, list open tasks, and ask questions about a meeting. **Read-only — nothing with a token can delete or change a recording.** The token is stored in the iPhone's keychain, never leaves it except when you copy it, and revoking it cuts off everything immediately, including anything mid-stream. Full reference in [docs/api.md](docs/api.md).
- **Find tasks in older recordings.** Task extraction runs automatically on everything new, but recordings transcribed before the Tasks tab existed had never been scanned. The Tasks menu now offers to go back over them, one at a time with a progress bar. It only appears when there's something to scan.
- **A Tasks tab.** Action items are pulled out of each recording after it's transcribed and collected in one place, grouped by the recording they came from. Tick them off, swipe to delete, tap through to the recording, or add one by hand. Deadlines are kept as they were spoken ("by Friday") rather than guessed at as dates — the on-device model has no reliable idea what today is, and a wrong date in a task list is worse than a phrase. **Re-transcribing a recording never resurrects an item you've ticked off.** Works with items you add yourself even on iPhones without Apple Intelligence, where the automatic extraction quietly does nothing.
- **Recordings can be named after the meeting they happened in.** Switch on Settings › Recorder › Calendar and Bounce looks for an event overlapping the recording — in any calendar on the phone, including Google and Exchange accounts — and uses its title, plus its attendees as suggestions when you name speakers. It only names recordings you haven't titled yourself, never writes to your calendar, and nothing leaves the phone. Off by default; permission is asked the first time you switch it on.
- **Tags.** A recording can carry several tags at once, and the Library can filter to recordings that have *all* of the ones you pick — which is the thing folders can't do, since a recording only fits in one folder. Tags are stored by identity, so renaming one keeps every recording attached to it, and deleting one clears it from every recording rather than leaving an invisible label behind.
- **Speaker names you've used before are offered when you name speakers**, with a recording's calendar attendees offered first. When a recording has the same number of speakers as your last one in the same category, and you'd named all of them, Bounce pre-fills those names in the same order — shown greyed and unconfirmed until you accept them, because it's a guess from position. **Bounce does not recognise voices, and no transcription engine it uses can**; the UI says so plainly rather than implying otherwise. Manage the list in Settings › Transcription › Known speakers.
- **Auto-organize**: after each transcription, Apple Intelligence classifies the recording into a category (Meeting, Task, Reminder, Note — or any the user defines), titles untitled recordings with the category's prefix ("Meeting: Budget review"), and generates that category's summary templates automatically — all on device, before automatic delivery, and skipped cleanly on hardware without Apple Intelligence. Switchable in Settings › AI.
- **User-defined categories**: each category carries a name, a title prefix, guidance that teaches the AI when it applies, and the summary templates to auto-run for it. Editable, deletable, and extendable in Settings, with a restorable starter set.
- **Every summary template is editable** — built-ins included — from a new Settings › AI › Templates screen. Edited built-ins can be reset to their shipped version, and any template can be marked to run automatically for every new recording regardless of category.
- **Name speakers**: recordings transcribed with speaker diarization (Soniox) can map "Speaker 1/2" labels to real names from the recording's actions menu. Names appear in the transcript view and in every export — share sheet, webhook, and folder. Per recording, since the engine's speaker labels aren't stable across recordings.
- **Soniox language hints**: an optional list of languages you expect to speak (e.g. "en, es"), sent with both live and post-sync requests to improve accuracy for multilingual users.
- **Soniox custom vocabulary**: names, jargon, and product terms Soniox should recognize, configurable in Settings and sent as transcription context with every request.
- **Soniox language identification**: transcripts are now tagged with the language actually detected in the audio rather than the language that was requested.
- **Soniox live translation**: an optional target language that translates the live preview in real time as you speak. The authoritative post-sync transcript stays in the original language.
- Lower live-caption latency on Soniox: endpoint detection finalizes text as soon as the speaker pauses instead of holding it as provisional.
- A recording **Live Activity**: elapsed time on the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island with Pause/Resume and Stop controls, so you can manage a recording without opening the app.
- A persistent in-app **recording indicator** docked above the tab bar while recording — live timer, pause/stop, and tap to reopen the recording screen — reachable from any tab.
- An **Ask** tab that searches and answers questions across all your transcribed recordings on device, showing the related recordings it drew from.
- A **Highlight** button on the recording screen to flag moments as you record; the marks appear as tappable seek points on the recording afterwards.
- Ask questions about a recording's transcript and get answers from **Apple Intelligence, on device** — in the recording detail view and live while recording. Uses the Foundation Models framework (iOS 26); the transcript and questions never leave the phone. Available on Apple-Intelligence-capable hardware; elsewhere it explains why it's unavailable. Long transcripts are answered from their most recent portion, a limit of the on-device model's context window.
- The live-while-recording transcript is now **archived** rather than discarded when the authoritative post-sync pass replaces it, viewable as a collapsed "Live draft" in the recording detail.
- An optional **Soniox** cloud transcription engine, selectable in Settings for both live and post-sync transcription. On-device (Apple) remains the default; Soniox trades higher accuracy and more languages for sending recording audio to Soniox's servers, which Settings states plainly. The API key is held in the keychain like the Plaud credentials, and if Soniox is unreachable or unconfigured Bounce falls back to on-device automatically so a transcript is never lost.
- A "Delete from recorder after syncing" setting. This already happened unconditionally — matching the reference app — but it destroys data on the device, so it is now visible and switchable. Still on by default, and deletion still only happens after the audio is on disk and recorded in the library.
- An app icon, generated by `tools/make-icon.swift` so it stays diffable and editable rather than being an opaque binary asset.
- Redacted logging across the authentication path (`Auth/AuthLog`), debug builds only. Secrets are fingerprinted by length and a two-character prefix, and tokens by length alone, so a log line can never carry a usable credential. The auth flow was previously silent and impossible to diagnose from the console.
- Self-renewing authentication. Bounce stores your Plaud Client ID and Secret Key in the iPhone keychain and mints its own access tokens on demand, so it keeps working indefinitely with no backend. Plaud user tokens last about 24 hours and have no refresh mechanism, so an app holding only a token would have stopped working after a day.
- First-launch credential entry, verified against Plaud before being saved so a typo can't leave the app in a broken state. Region selectable between US and Japan.
- Face ID / passcode gate on viewing or changing stored credentials, and a Settings row showing when the access token next renews.
- Bounce, a SwiftUI app for iOS 26 that pulls recordings off a Plaud recorder, transcribes them on the device, and sends them onward. Lives in `app/`.
- Live transcription while the recorder is still recording, on device. Text appears within a few seconds of speech and is replaced by the higher-quality pass over the complete file once it syncs. Experimental, and switchable in Settings: the recorder never sends decoded audio during a recording, so Bounce reassembles and decrypts the raw stream itself, decoding it incrementally in memory as it arrives, which costs battery and produces a lossier transcript than the final one.
- On-device transcription using Apple's `SpeechAnalyzer` and `SpeechTranscriber`. Audio never leaves the phone to become text, and no speech API key is required. Language models install on demand and are shared with other apps.
- Per-phrase transcript timestamps, so tapping a line seeks the audio player to that point.
- Send recordings to a webhook as a single `multipart/form-data` POST carrying metadata JSON, transcript text, and the audio file, with an optional `X-Bounce-Secret` shared secret.
- Auto-export of audio and transcript to a chosen folder in Files or iCloud Drive.
- Native share sheet on every recording, covering Mail, Messages, and any installed app.
- Five Shortcuts and Siri actions — Get Latest Transcript, Get Transcript, Transcribe Recording, Send Recording, and Sync Recorder — so automations can route recordings anywhere.
- Optional automatic pipeline: sync, then transcribe, then deliver to every configured destination, with no interaction.
- Settings for transcription language, transcript style, payload contents, and destinations.
- Library with day grouping, full-text search across transcripts, and filtering by transcription state.
- `docs/architecture.md` and `docs/development.md`.
- **A logo on launch instead of a white screen.** Bounce now shows its mark on a matching background for the moment between tapping the icon and the app appearing, in light and dark mode, rather than a blank white page.
- **Correct a misheard word everywhere at once.** "Correct a word…" in a recording's ⋯ menu replaces every occurrence in the transcript in one go, and tells you how many it will change before you commit. Matching is by whole word, so correcting "AI" never touches "said". A capitalised occurrence stays capitalised, so a word at the start of a sentence isn't lowercased. Timings aren't touched, so tap-to-seek keeps working. If you're using Soniox, it offers to add the correction to your custom vocabulary so future recordings get it right — which is the difference between fixing a name once and fixing it forever. Summaries are deliberately left alone; they were written from the old text, and the sheet says so when there are any.
- **A calendar view of the library.** A toolbar button switches the Library between the list and a month grid showing which days have recordings; tap a day to see just that day, tap it again to clear. Month names and week start follow your system locale.
- **Low battery alerts for the recorder.** Settings › Recorder › Battery notifies you once when the recorder drops below 10%, 20% or 30%. Once per drop, not repeatedly — it stays quiet until the recorder has charged back up, so a battery hovering on the threshold doesn't nag. Off by default, and permission is asked the first time you switch it on rather than at launch. The footer is explicit that Bounce has to be running to see the reading, so it won't catch a battery that runs down days after you last opened the app.
- **Markdown transcripts, for Obsidian and Logseq.** A third transcript style in Settings › Transcription writes a `.md` note with YAML frontmatter (title, date, duration, category, speakers), your summaries as sections, and the transcript as speaker-labelled paragraphs. Combined with the existing Files/iCloud destination, recordings drop straight into a vault. Titles containing a colon or a quote are quoted correctly, and a transcript line starting with `---` can no longer terminate the frontmatter and wreck the note.
- The desk view's waveform now colours correctly in both schemes — it previously sampled custom properties whose value can be an unevaluated `light-dark()`, which the canvas rejects — and the played portion is clearly distinguishable from the rest.
- Desk-view library rows no longer render before categories have loaded and stay uncoloured.

### Changed

- **The Recorder screen leads with your recordings, not your recorder.** Today's recordings are a deck of cards you swipe through — each showing its waveform, category, length and how many tasks came out of it — over a one-line device strip and a single Record control. The old screen opened on a device card whose largest line was flash usage, above a full-width slab, above rows that showed only a date.
- **The live recording screen is a console.** Dark, with the elapsed time as the largest thing on it, a scrolling ribbon of the last few seconds of input level, the transcript beneath, and three separated transport controls instead of one segmented pill. The level ribbon still only appears when live transcription is actually decoding audio — with it off there is nothing real to draw, and Bounce doesn't draw a fake one.
- **The Library opens on a timeline.** Each recording hangs off a day spine with its bar height set by its length, so a day's shape is visible at a glance rather than assembled by reading every row. List, Calendar, Map and Series are still in the view menu.
- **The Summary tab only shows summaries that exist.** Templates you haven't run were each drawing a full card containing nothing but a Generate button, so a recording with one summary opened on three empty boxes above it. They're now a single **Add a summary** row whose menu lists what's left, with a Run-all shortcut.
- **Ask keeps the conversation.** Questions and answers stack up, each with the recordings it drew from, instead of one answer being replaced by the next. Each question is still answered independently — this isn't a chat with memory, and the field says "Ask another question" rather than implying otherwise.
- **Settings opens with a status card** — recorder, transcription engine, delivery destinations and desktop view, in one place — instead of seven right-aligned grey values you had to read down the page and assemble yourself. Delivery destinations are named rather than counted.
- **Tasks look like the recordings they came from.** Each group is headed by its recording's category glyph, title and date with a done/total chip, and a task shows the timecode where it was said. Swipe right to complete or reopen.
- **One shared set of UI pieces across these screens.** Section labels, status dots, chips, speaker avatars and sparklines are now defined once, so the screens read as one app rather than six.

- **Settings is now seven category pages** — Recorder Hardware, Audio & Recording, Transcription & Engine, AI & Intelligence, Integrations & Delivery, Desktop View & API, and Plaud Account — with everything irreversible kept together in one Danger zone on the root screen. Calendar matching, task calendar events, Reminders sync, folder export, webhooks and Shortcuts now sit together in one Integrations page instead of being scattered.
- **Long transcripts are summarised from both ends.** Anything past the on-device model's budget used to be cut down to just its last few thousand characters, which threw away why a meeting was called and what its agenda was. Summaries, titles, action items and Ask now see the opening *and* the close, with the removed middle marked so the model can tell something was cut.
- **The recording detail screen leads with its title** — a serif display header with date, duration, and word count as one quiet line beneath, replacing the boxed three-column stat row. Transcript paragraphs are set in the same reading face with opened-up line spacing, matching the desk view's typography.
- **Opening a recording now zooms from its row** (the iOS 26 zoom navigation transition) from the Library, Home, and Ask screens, and falls back to the standard push when Reduce Motion is on.
- **Highlights have their own colour**: a warm amber for the marks on the waveform and the highlight chips — the same amber the desk view uses for position and marks — instead of everything sharing the one brand blue. A `tools/check-brand-colors.swift` checker now guards the brand colours against drifting apart across their five copies — the app, the icon and launch-logo scripts, and both asset catalogs — and flags a colorset that silently changes colour space.
- **The desk view got a full redesign.** A dark-first studio look with layered surfaces, one violet accent, and glass chrome: the transport is now a floating glass pill the transcript scrolls beneath, day headers blur what passes under them, and rows, tabs, and buttons picked up depth and motion. Selecting a recording or switching tabs animates via the View Transitions API, and popovers and the toast ease in rather than popping. A theme switch in the top rail forces light or dark independent of the system. All of it degrades cleanly: no blur without `backdrop-filter`, no motion under Reduce Motion, solid surfaces under Reduce Transparency.
- **Categories are visible in the desk view**: each library row carries its category's colour as a dot, and the reader header shows a tinted category chip — the same ten swatches the phone uses, including the same deterministic colour for categories you haven't styled.
- **The app's logo now appears throughout the desk view** — as the browser tab's favicon, in the top rail next to the wordmark, on the pairing card, and in empty states — drawn as inline SVG from the same five-bar geometry as `tools/make-icon.swift`, so it costs no extra request to the phone.
- On a phone-sized window the desk view now opens on the recordings list instead of an empty reader, and the top rail no longer overflows.
- **The bar above the tab bar only appears when there's something in it.** It was reserving its slot and drawing an empty capsule whenever nothing was recording, syncing, or transcribing.
- Skip buttons moved from ±10s to **±15s**, matching the interval every other spoken-audio player uses and the one the Lock Screen and AirPods controls now offer.
- **Settings reorganized into category pages** (Transcription, AI, Delivery, Recorder, Plaud account) reached from a short list of rows, instead of one long scroll through a dozen-plus sections. Irreversible actions (Danger zone) and version info (About) stay visible at the root.
- Settings' transcription and Soniox sections were split so every explanation lives in a section footer instead of as a plain row — and the Soniox API key now saves once, on submit or when you tap away, instead of on every keystroke, with a visible message if the save fails.
- **Recorder device management** in Settings: rename the recorder, toggle USB drive mode, erase all recordings on the recorder, and factory reset it — each behind a destructive confirmation dialog, and disabled while a sync is in flight. Factory reset unpairs the recorder from Bounce immediately rather than waiting for a device response.
- **Idle WiFi sync** (experimental): store WiFi networks on the recorder in Settings › Recorder settings › Idle WiFi Sync so it can sync recordings on its own schedule. Marked experimental in the UI because whether this uploads to Plaud's cloud or somewhere this app controls hasn't yet been confirmed on real hardware.
- **Recording quality settings** in a new "Recording quality" Settings section: scene, mode, voice activation and its sensitivity, and microphone processing gain. Everything except mic gain is sent to the recorder without confirmation back, so the UI is explicit that it shows the last value Bounce requested, not verified device state, until you pick one.
- Settings reorganized: "Recorder" now separates read-only device info from a new "Recorder settings" section, and every irreversible action (erase recordings, factory reset, unpair, remove credentials) is consolidated into a single "Danger zone" section instead of being scattered next to unrelated info.
- Redesigned the active-recording screen: the live transcript is now the focus and fills the screen, the timer and status shrank to a slim top bar, and the two large circular buttons became one compact Pause · Stop · Highlight segmented control. The AI Q&A moved into a pull-up sheet so it no longer crowds the transcript.
- WiFi Fast Transfer now ships disabled, behind a single switch in `project.yml`. Its entitlements require a paid Apple Developer Program membership and cannot be signed by a free Personal Team, which failed the build outright. As a result Bounce currently requests no location permission at all, since that was only ever needed to join the recorder's hotspot. Everything else — Bluetooth sync, on-device transcription, all delivery destinations, Shortcuts — is unaffected.
- Rebuilt the interface in SwiftUI against the iOS 26 Liquid Glass design language, replacing the UIKit interface that targeted iOS 14 and simulated glass with a `UIVisualEffectView` blur. Glass is confined to the navigation layer and falls back to a material when Reduce Transparency is on.
- Raised the minimum iOS version from 14.0 to 26.0, required by `SpeechAnalyzer`.
- Moved the app from `plaud-template-app/ios/` to `app/`, and preserved Plaud's original UIKit sample at `reference/plaud-template-app-uikit/`.
- WiFi Fast Transfer now exports MP3 rather than Opus. Opus lands in an Ogg container that `AVAudioFile` cannot read, which would have made WiFi-synced recordings impossible to transcribe on device.
- Guarded `RecordingManager`'s waveform level with a lock. It is written on the Bluetooth callback queue and read by a main-queue timer, which was an unsynchronised cross-queue access.

### Removed

- All credentials from the build. `PartnerConfig.xcconfig` is gone, along with the `UserAccessToken` key in `Info.plist`, so nothing sensitive exists in a tracked file or in the built app. Credentials are keychain-only, entered at runtime, stored device-only, never synced to iCloud, and excluded from backups.
- Cloud transcription: `PlaudAPIService`, `TranscriptionManager`, the S3 multipart upload, and the polling loop. Transcription is now local.
- The `PLAUD_CLIENT_ID` and `PLAUD_API_KEY` credentials, which existed only for the cloud transcription API.
- The `PARTNER_TOKEN` / `PartnerToken` deprecated credential alias.
- `MockDeviceManager`, `MockSyncManager`, and `MockRecordingManager`, which were never wired to anything.

### Fixed

- **Leaving Bounce mid-recording no longer restarts the live transcript from the top of the call.** The transcript is now saved to the phone as it's written, so coming back — even after iOS has closed the app behind you — picks up where it left off instead of transcribing the meeting a second time from the beginning. It resumes at the last thing that had been transcribed, so the few seconds that were still being worked out aren't lost either, and it continues from the point in the recording it had reached rather than re-fetching the whole call over Bluetooth. The saved text also means a live transcript survives a crash in the gap between the recording ending and its audio syncing.
- **Pausing a recording no longer kills the live transcript.** Resuming used to leave it frozen at the moment you paused, with nothing arriving until the recording finished syncing: the audio stream from the recorder ends silently on a pause and nothing restarted it. Bounce now re-opens it on resume — from where it left off, so the transcript catches up in seconds rather than re-fetching the whole recording. If the cloud engine's connection timed out while you were paused, it reconnects too, keeping everything already transcribed and continuing on the right timeline instead of going quiet for the rest of the recording.
- **The elapsed time after resuming a recording showed an absurd number.** Resume anchored the timer to 1970, so it read tens of thousands of hours. It now keeps the recording's real start time across any number of pauses.
- **Asking about "today" could be answered with another day's recording.** If nothing was recorded today, the question quietly fell back to searching the whole library and answered confidently from whatever matched. It now says nothing was recorded then, rather than answering about the wrong day. "This week" also means the actual calendar week now, not the last seven days.
- **Ask listed recordings it hadn't actually read.** With more matches than fit the model's context, the sources under an answer included ones that were never sent to it.
- **Notes to yourself weren't becoming tasks.** A recording saying "remember to take out the trash" produced no action item at all: the AI was being asked for things "somebody committed to doing" with a named owner, which described meetings and nothing else. It now knows a recording may be you talking to yourself, and that a note like that is a task.
- **Short recordings were being named after meetings that happened to overlap them.** An 8-second reminder recorded during a meeting was titled after that meeting. Bounce now checks the recording plausibly covers the event before borrowing its name, and never does so for recordings under a minute. Categories can also opt out entirely — useful for Reminder and Note, where a meeting name is never right.
- **Several recordings of the same meeting are now numbered** "(Pt 2)", "(Pt 3)" instead of sharing one title.
- **A failed auto-summary was saved and sent as if it were real.** When the on-device model couldn't produce a summary during the auto-organize pass, its "Couldn't generate this summary…" apology was stored on the recording and included in the webhook/folder delivery as genuine output. Bounce now discards a failed summary instead of persisting or delivering the apology text.
- **Recordings usually failed to link to the right calendar meeting.** A recording is timestamped by the *recorder's* clock, which drifts from your phone's; when the drift was more than a few minutes, nothing in your calendar lined up and no meeting was linked — so it worked once and rarely again. Rather than silently shifting timestamps (which would mis-assign back-to-back meetings), Bounce now links automatically only when it's genuinely confident and gives you a picker of the surrounding meetings for every other case, so a missed match is one tap to fix — and the fix sticks.
- **The recents list didn't refresh after a recording was auto-titled.** The new title only appeared after a pull-to-refresh, though opening the recording showed it correctly. It affected AI titles, categories and summaries too, not just calendar-derived names.
- **Tasks can mirror into Apple Reminders.** Switch it on in Settings › Transcription › Reminders and every task Bounce finds is added to a Reminders list of your choosing — and ticking one off in *either* app updates the other. Only tasks Bounce created are touched; it never edits or deletes anything else, and deleting a reminder in the Reminders app simply unlinks it rather than bringing it back. Deadlines stay as they were spoken ("by Friday") in the reminder's notes rather than being guessed at as a date, because a reminder that fires on the wrong day is worse than one that doesn't fire. Off by default — it writes into another app's data — and it needs its own permission, separate from calendar access.
- **A library saved before a field was added could fail to load entirely.** `deliveredTo` was stored in a way that required the value to be present, so a library written before delivery tracking existed couldn't be read — and because the whole library is loaded in one go, that meant *all* recordings, not one. Fixed, with the original data preserved where it exists, and there's now an automated check that loads libraries from every past version of the format so this can't recur.
- **The live transcript now reads like a transcript**, not a wall of text. Speech is grouped into blocks by speaker and by pauses in the conversation, with the time running down the left-hand side, so you can see the shape of what's been said as it happens. Scroll up to read back and the view stops chasing the speaker; a "Jump to live" button brings you back to the moment. Blocks look the same here as they do once a recording has synced, so nothing shifts under you when the final transcript replaces the live one.
- **Name the voices while you're still recording**, from the desktop view — mid-meeting is when you actually know who's talking. The names are held against the session and attached to the recording when it syncs off the recorder. Diarization ids are assigned per transcription pass, so on a long recording the carry-over is best-effort; correct it in the detail view if a name lands on the wrong voice.
- **A recording in progress is now just the top item in your desktop library**, marked live, instead of living behind a separate Live tab. Open it and you get the same reading view as any other recording — the transcript arriving as it's spoken, and Ask, so you can put a question to a meeting while it's still happening. The Live tab is gone; there's one list to look at.
- **Audio editing.** "Edit audio" in a recording's ⋯ menu opens an editor where you can trim a recording down to a range, cut a range out of the middle, or have Bounce find the silent gaps and remove them in one tap. Drag the two handles or nudge them a half-second at a time; tap a segment to select it, and use the transport to jump to its start or end and audition just that part before committing. Undo and redo throughout. **Saving writes a new recording and leaves the original completely untouched**, so an edit can't lose anything. Editing is lossless: the audio is copied frame by frame rather than re-encoded, so there's no quality loss and no wait — the tradeoff is that cuts land on a frame boundary, accurate to about 36 milliseconds.
- **An edited copy keeps its transcript.** Timings are re-mapped onto the shortened audio so tap-to-seek still lands on the right sentence; phrases whose audio was mostly removed are dropped, and highlight marks are re-mapped the same way. So a trimmed recording is immediately readable instead of needing to be transcribed again — though you can ask for that too, from the save screen.
- **The desktop view follows the audio phrase by phrase**, the way the iPhone's own transcript does: the phrase being spoken is marked, the rest of the paragraph recedes, and the page keeps it in view. Click any phrase to play from there.
- **The desktop view is encrypted by default.** Bounce mints its own certificate on the iPhone and serves HTTPS, so nothing else on the network can read your recordings, audio, or pairing code as they cross it. Your browser will warn you the first time and you'll need to continue past it — no one can issue a trusted certificate for a home network address — and Settings shows the certificate's fingerprint so you can check it against what the browser reports. Encryption can be turned off in Settings › Desktop view if you'd rather not see the warning, with a plain statement of what that costs.
- **Ask works in the desktop view too**, both across the whole library (the Ask tab) and about a single recording. The question goes to your iPhone, the answer streams back as it's written, and the recordings it drew on are listed underneath so you can jump straight to one. Same on-device Apple Intelligence model as on the phone — nothing is uploaded.
- **Desktop view.** Switch it on in Settings › Desktop view and Bounce serves your library to a web browser on the same network — read transcripts on a full-size screen, play recordings, retitle them, name speakers, set categories, run summary templates, and watch a live transcript as you record. Nothing goes to a cloud; the iPhone is the server and the data never leaves your network. Bounce has to stay open and on screen while it runs, because iOS suspends backgrounded apps and that closes the connection. Off by default; a browser has to enter a six-digit code shown on the phone, and you can see and revoke connected browsers in Settings. Traffic on the network isn't encrypted — a browser can't be given a trusted certificate for a local address — so it's for networks you trust.
- **A real audio player.** The recording detail's transport is now a waveform rather than a slider: the recording's actual shape, drawn from its peaks, scrubbable by dragging, with your highlight marks shown in place along it. The bar has two states — collapsed to a waveform, play/pause, position and speed; expanded (tap the chevron) to add ±15s skips and a large play control — so the transcript keeps the screen.
- **Playback speed**, from 0.75× to 2×, which is the difference between re-listening to a meeting and re-attending it.
- **Lock Screen, Control Centre, AirPods and CarPlay playback controls**, and playback that continues with the phone locked. Note these appear only when no recorder is connected: while one is, Bounce shares the audio session with it (which is what keeps playback working at all) and a shared session can't own the Lock Screen.
- **The transcript follows the audio**, highlighting the phrase being spoken and scrolling to keep it in view while playing. It uses the per-phrase timings transcripts already carried; previously only the surrounding minute was highlighted.
- **A waveform on every library row**, so recordings are recognizable by shape rather than only by title and timestamp. Envelopes are computed once and cached, in the background, a few at a time.
- **Categories are now visible.** Every category carries a colour and a glyph, shown against each recording in the library and on the recording itself — so a list of recordings is scannable rather than uniform. Pick them per category in Settings › AI › Categories; a category you haven't styled still gets a consistent colour of its own rather than defaulting to grey. The recording's category was already being worked out automatically after each transcription; it just wasn't shown anywhere.
- **Filter the library by category**, from a new toolbar control next to search.
- **iOS 26 Liquid Glass adoption pass**: Library and Home's recent recordings are now real `List`s with swipe-to-delete/transcribe and native section headers; the record button gets real press feedback; the recording detail's player bar and tab picker float as proper glass bars that fade the content scrolling beneath them; the recording screen's live level meter now reflects actual microphone input (when live transcription is on) instead of a synthetic pulse; the Ask input field stays docked at the bottom instead of scrolling away; and Dynamic Type, Reduce Motion, and VoiceOver are handled consistently across the app (scaled icon columns and control heights, motion-sensitive pulse effects, and labeled status indicators). Brand colour now reaches the widget and Lock Screen Live Activity, which previously rendered system blue.
- **The recorder's storage reading now updates as things change.** It was only ever read when the recorder connected, so it never went down after a sync — which made it look as though recordings weren't being deleted from the recorder when they were. It now refreshes when a recording stops, when a sync finishes, when the recorder confirms a deletion, and when you erase all recordings — which previously gave you no feedback at all, since the recorder doesn't acknowledge it. The line also states how many recordings are still waiting to sync, because the bytes figure covers everything on the device, not just your recordings: an empty recorder still reports tens of megabytes in use, and each recording it has ever held costs a fixed 64 KB whether it was a minute or an hour long.
- **`<end>` no longer appears in live transcripts.** Soniox emits it as an endpoint marker — it's why phrases break where they do — and it was being written into the text as if it were a word. It also had no timing, so it landed at 0:00 and pushed the rest of the transcript's timeline out of order. Recordings that already picked it up are cleaned up when the library loads, so old live drafts read correctly too.
- **The live transcript no longer rebuilds itself several times a second.** Every refinement of the phrase being spoken was redrawing the entire transcript above it, and the whole thing was built eagerly, so the longer a recording ran the more work each word cost.
- **Desktop view's idle timer no longer runs while you're recording.** Walking away from the desk mid-meeting was switching the server off, which is the one moment it most needs to still be there when you come back.
- **Playback intermittently failed with "Couldn't start audio playback. (OSStatus error -50.)"** It happened whenever no recorder was connected — which is the usual case for listening back, and why it looked random: the same recording played fine right after a sync and failed once Bluetooth dropped. Bounce was asking for the playback audio session with two route options that `.playback` already implies; iOS 26 rejects that combination outright where earlier versions ignored it. Failures now also log the actual status code and session state, since a bare "(OSStatus error -50.)" gave nothing to act on.
- **Stopping playback could be held up indefinitely** behind an audio-session call still blocked in the media server, leaving the session active and the Lock Screen controls wired to a screen the user had already left. The wait is now bounded, as the matching wait on the start path already was.
- **Timecodes past one hour were wrong everywhere.** A 92-minute recording showed "92:14" instead of "1:32:14" — in the player, the transcript, and the highlight marks. Four copies of the same minutes-only formatter; there is now one, and it renders `h:mm:ss` past the hour. **This changes exported transcript text for recordings over an hour**, under the default "timecoded" transcript format: the webhook body, the webhook's attached `.txt`, the folder/iCloud export, and the share-sheet `.txt` all now read `[1:32:14]` where they read `[92:14]`. Shortcuts output is unaffected (it sends plain text, no timecodes), as is the webhook's JSON metadata (raw seconds).
- **A paused recording past an hour showed the wrong elapsed time on the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island**, and changed format the moment you paused it — the running state rolled over to `h:mm:ss` on its own while the paused state was pinned to minutes and seconds.
- **Live translation showed a blank preview whenever the target language already matched the spoken language.** Soniox tags those tokens `translation_status: "none"` (nothing needed translating), which the live-preview filter discarded along with the paired source-language tokens, leaving the screen stuck on "Listening…" with no error. It now shows `"none"` tokens as-is and only discards the paired source-language token, so the preview never goes blank whether or not translation actually happens.
- **`DEBUG` was never defined in the Debug configuration**, in either build. `project.yml` set `SWIFT_ACTIVE_COMPILATION_CONDITIONS` at the base level (applying identically to Debug and Release) to turn on WiFi Fast Transfer, which meant nothing else ever added `DEBUG` for Debug builds — silently compiling out every `#if DEBUG` diagnostic log (`TranscribeLog`, `DeviceLog`, `AuthLog`) app-wide, in both configurations, since the app's first XcodeGen-based build. The setting now lives in a per-configuration block so Debug gets `DEBUG` and Release doesn't.
- On-device Q&A answers now read as plain sentences. The model sometimes replied with raw JSON (e.g. `{"answer": "…"}`); it's now instructed to answer in natural prose only.
- Recordings play. Playback completion was inferred by polling `AVAudioPlayer.isPlaying` from the progress ticker, which raced with playback starting — the first tick could land before the audio engine reported itself playing, so the UI snapped back to 0:00 and nothing was ever heard. Completion now comes from the delegate. The audio session is also claimed at play time rather than at load, since the SDK uses it while streaming from the recorder, and playback errors are shown instead of failing silently.
- On-device transcription works. Three distinct faults, all in Bounce rather than in the audio:
  - The read loop ran one chunk past the end of the file. `AVAudioFile.read(into:)` throws at end-of-file rather than returning zero frames, so an unbounded loop always made one read too many, failed with an opaque `_GenericObjCError.nilError`, and tore down the analyzer before it could finalise — every run reported zero segments. The loop is now bounded by `audioFile.length`.
  - The drive and finalise calls were mismatched: `start(inputSequence:)` spins up the analyzer's own autonomous input-loop worker, and calling `finalizeAndFinishThroughEndOfInput()` alongside it raced that worker (`Failed precondition: Attempt to modify worker after it was locked`). Now uses Apple's documented pairing, `analyzeSequence(_:)` → `finalizeAndFinish(through: lastSampleTime)`.
  - The locale was only reserved with `AssetInventory` when the speech model needed downloading. A reservation is what permits *using* a module, not merely installing it, so with the model already present the analyzer was never allocated and produced nothing.
- A damaged final MP3 packet no longer discards a whole transcript. MP3 frame counts are estimates, so the reported length can overshoot the real end; a tail read failure now keeps whatever decoded.
- Errors on the transcription path name the stage that failed, and log the error's type. Speech framework failures like `nilError` describe neither domain nor cause, which made several of the above look like audio corruption.
- NotePro "Find My" recorders are now discoverable. The SDK's scan filters on BLE service `0x1910`, but these units advertise `0x504C`, so `bleScanResult` never fired and the device could not be found at all — no error, just an empty list. Bounce now also scans unfiltered and reconstructs devices from the advertisement's manufacturer data, which the SDK's own `BleDevice` parser reads correctly. Connection and everything after it are unchanged.
- Requested access-token lifetimes are now capped at 24 hours. Plaud's documentation states no maximum for `expires_in`, but the API rejects anything above 86400 with HTTP 422, so the initial 30-day request failed outright and no credentials could be saved. Established by testing the live endpoint.
- The SDK's `PlaudDeviceBasicSDK.bundle` now actually reaches the app bundle. It was declared under a `resources:` key in `project.yml`, which XcodeGen silently ignores; it needed to be under `sources:` with `buildPhase: resources`. The upstream template carries the same defect.
